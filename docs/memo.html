1 — google ads

we are paying to buy people who will never pay us

we bid on loom keywords. that part is fine as an idea. the numbers are not.

"loom alternative" costs $13.35 a click in the US and has 1,000 searches a month. even if it converted perfectly it does not scale. there is no volume behind it.

but that is not the real problem. the real problem is what we told google to optimise for.

our campaigns run maximize conversions on the free sign_up. google is doing exactly what we asked. it goes and finds the people most likely to sign up for something free. those are the people least likely to ever pay us. we are training the machine against ourselves.

and the purchase event probably never fires — no firebase, no stripe.js, billing is our own backend. so payments can read as zero even when they happen. no feedback loop at all.

this was in our own audit in may

the may conversion audit already said it: "bidding is trained to find people who will never pay" and "the purchase event likely never fires." we didn't act on it. that's on me, not on anyone here.

so the order is: fix tracking, then bid

server-side trial_start and value-based purchase into GTM → GA4 → Ads. verify one real upgrade end to end. then re-point bidding at purchase, demote sign-up to secondary.

until that is done, every extra dollar in google is spent blind. we don't scale spend before we can see.

where google still makes sense

same keyword, other markets: brazil $1.08, india $0.91, philippines $1.25, poland $1.23, south africa $1.33. against $13.35 in the US. that's 10–15× cheaper for the same intent. if we keep paid search, it belongs there.

2 — our own pricing

we stop at 25 videos. so does loom.

i read 1,795 real user comments about loom — play store, app store, reddit, hacker news. the loudest single complaint in the whole set is loom's free cap: 25 videos, 5 minutes. people quote that number when they say why they left.

our free plan also stops at 25 videos. so we run ads attacking loom for a limit we also have, and everyone we send lands on a pricing page showing them the same wall they just walked away from.

we cap on the 5GB storage instead. nobody complains about storage. then "no video wall" is something we can actually say.

the price comparison we're not making anywhere

$71.99/year vs loom business at $180/user/year. 60% cheaper, unlimited recordings, dubbing included. that's a strong line and it is nowhere in our marketing.

and the homepage

"pay loom to record — or record and get paid with boom" plus "free forever" eight times. across 1,795 complaints not one person asked to be paid for recording. they asked for a tool that doesn't lose their work.

the may audit reached the same conclusion independently: rewards-first copy "recruits reward-hunters, the users least likely to pay." two separate analyses, same answer. we should believe it now.

3 — the plot

a screen recorder is boring on social. a memory game is not.

i scraped 360 tiktok videos and 770 X posts to check this rather than guess.

the best screen-recorder creator i could find pulls about 37,000 views a post. a memory-game creator with half the followers pulls 5,850,000. that is 158×. it is not a content-quality gap, it is a different category of demand. a recorder is a considered purchase explained to a small audience. a memory game is a reflex anyone plays in three seconds.

the memory niche does 4 to 30 million views a video. and here is the part that matters: not one account in that niche is a company. they are quiz pages, hobbyists, family channels. nobody in there has anything to sell.

so there is a huge format with no product in it, and a product category with no format. we are the only ones who can be in both, because we already have a recorder and a memory game with cash prizes.

screen recall — the thing we build

you record 15 seconds of any screen. boom turns it into a memory challenge. score, leaderboard, cash prize. the share link is the game.

to play you have to record. so the recorder is the door, not the pitch. and every memory game out there uses generic cards and emojis — a screen is personal and different every single time, and people want to see other people's screens.

and it fixes a real product problem

from the research: people don't watch demos. 15-second median on a 2-minute walkthrough. that is the actual unsolved problem in screen recording.

a recall challenge is a test of whether you watched. so the game doesn't just market the recorder — it makes the recorder work better. that's the two-in-one, and it's why this isn't just a stunt.

what could make it fail — say it out loud

memory-game viewers are not shopping for a screen recorder. this only pays back because boom match sells credits at a $5–20 impulse. if credit conversion off cold viral traffic is near zero, this is expensive awareness and nothing else.

nobody knows that number yet. that's exactly what the $4,550 buys — after wave 1 it's arithmetic instead of an argument. we do not scale it before we have that number.

5 — two funnels

don't mix the messages

funnel 1 — volume

the game

"can you recall this screen?"

social, tiktok first. lands on a game. money is credits, $5–20 impulse. this is where the reach is. rewards and prizes belong here and only here.

funnel 2 — revenue per head

the recorder

"it will not lose your recording."

loom refugees. SEO, comparison pages, builder creators on X. money is $71.99/year premium. no rewards language anywhere near this one.

this is the mistake we've been making

we serve funnel 1's message to funnel 2's traffic. someone who just lost a client recording reads "get paid to record" and decides we're a rewards app, not a tool. split the surfaces.

7 — honesty

what's measured and what's a bet

measured: the 1,795 user comments and every quote from them. the $13.35 CPC and the market volumes. competitor page counts from their own sitemaps. all the follower and view numbers — 360 tiktok videos and 770 X posts scraped 19 aug. the fact that no company is in the memory niche.

a bet, not a finding: screen recall itself. the name, the formats, the claim that it becomes a category. also that credit conversion off cold social traffic is high enough to pay for the creators. the creator fees are standard bands, not quotes — people will counter, and the big tiktok accounts may ask for several times these numbers.

wave 1 exists to turn the bet into a number for $4,550. that's the point of it.
