01 — two corrections

You were right on both, and the credit model is the bigger one

“The influencers will do it free” — wrong, and I should have known

Free product plus a referral bounty is an affiliate offer, and creators with real audiences decline affiliate offers. The market rate for a tech video post on X is $8–$20 per 1,000 followers, with a ~1.4× premium for video over plain text. Everything below is priced at that, and the whole page is rebuilt around paying up front.

“Users buy virtual credit for Boom Match” — this changes the strategy

In the last memo I warned that memory-game content pulls a huge, cheap audience who will never pay $71.99 for a screen recorder. That warning is now void. They don't need to buy a subscription — they buy credits.

Which means a game player is a directly monetised user, not an ad impression you hope converts later. The “low-intent” audience I flagged as a risk is now your second revenue line, and it converts on a $5–20 impulse instead of a $71.99 annual commitment. That's a far easier sale, and it makes paid creator posts justifiable in a way they weren't an hour ago.

So the funnel has two exits, not one. A person who sees a creator's post can become a Premium subscriber at $71.99/year, or a credit buyer at whatever a pack costs. You said you don't mind if they only ever do the second — that's the right instinct, because the second is where the volume is.

It also resolves the tension that has been running through this whole project. The reliability story sells subscriptions to Loom refugees. The memory game sells credits to everyone else. They are different audiences, different price points and different creative — but they share one product, one payment rail and one creator roster.

The number I still need from you

What a credit pack costs and what a buyer spends in their first month. Every break-even figure below is expressed as “revenue needed per paying user” precisely because I don't have it. Give me that one number and these tables become a go/no-go instead of a range.

Going from ten creators to fifty costs 15.8× more and buys 2.6× the views. The last fifteen names cost $12,098 and add 166,000 views — a CPM north of $70, which is worse than most paid media.

The tech-niche benchmark for sponsored content is a $10–40 CPM. The top-10 pack lands at $2.98. That is the entire argument for starting small: you are buying the most under-priced attention in the list, and you find out whether any of this works for two thousand dollars.

How I priced it, and where it's optimistic

Rate = followers ÷ 1,000 × $8–12 (banded by size) × 1.4 for video, floored at $60 and capped at $2,500.

Views come from the actual viewCount on each creator's qualifying post. But my scrape sorted by Top, so those are each creator's better posts, not their median. I applied a 40% haircut and capped any creator at 8× their follower count to stop one viral outlier distorting the model. Even so, treat these as the optimistic end — a real campaign will land below them.

Why the credit model makes this work

On the top-10 pack at a realistic 1% CTR and 5% pay rate, you need $23.87 per paying user. Against a $71.99 subscription that's comfortable. Against a credit pack it depends entirely on your pricing — but a buyer who spends $25 across their first month puts you at break-even on day one, with every repeat purchase pure margin.

At a 2% CTR the number drops to $11.93, which almost any credit pack clears on first purchase.

Keep a small bounty on top of the fee

Pay the post fee and $20 per referred paying user. The fee gets the post made; the bounty keeps them posting after the contract ends and tells you which creator actually drove revenue rather than just views.

All-in on the top-10 pack: $3,722 at a 1% CTR and 5% pay rate (85 payers, $43.87 each), or $5,419 at 2% / 5% (170 payers, $31.93 each). The bounty only grows when the campaign is working, which is the correct shape for a variable cost.

05 — the offer

What to send, now that it's paid

A paid brief is a different message from a free-tool pitch: lead with the fee, be specific about deliverable and deadline, and keep the creative in their voice.

1 · The offerDay 0 · X DM
Hey [name] — your [the thing they demoed] post is exactly the kind
of recording I'm building for.

Paid brief, no strings after it:
• $[fee] for one video post, your voice, your call on the angle
• Record it with Boom (free, I'll set you up with Premium)
• One link, one honest line about what it did or didn't do well
• Plus $20 per person who upgrades through your link, ongoing

Boom is a free screen recorder — auto-zoom on clicks, auto-captions,
dubs into 50+ languages. Happy to send it over so you can judge it
before you say yes.

Interested?
2 · Easier ask for the big accountsWhen the fee alone won't land it
Different idea, smaller lift.

We're launching Boom Match — a memory game with real cash prizes.
A round takes 15 seconds and it screen-records itself.

$[fee] for one post of you playing it and your score. That's it.
No product pitch, no talking about my software.

It performs well for you (score posts get replies), it takes you
30 seconds, and it does the job for me either way.
Why the second message exists

“Post about my screen recorder” is a hard ask that reads as an ad. “Post your score on this game” is a low-effort piece of content that performs for them — and it still shows Boom recording, in their feed, to their audience. For the 50k+ accounts it is usually the only version that gets a yes.

06 — how I'd spend the first $2,025

The sequence

Before

Tracked links, a payout date, and the 25-video cap gone

Every visitor these posts send lands on your pricing page and sees the same 25-video wall they left Loom over. Fix that first or you are paying to show people your weakest screen.

Week 1

Book the top 10 — $2,025, one post each

Half briefed on the recorder, half on Boom Match. That split is the actual experiment: which message converts cheaper. Everything after this decision should be informed by it rather than guessed.

Week 2

Measure CTR and cost per paying user, per creator

You now have a real click-through rate instead of my 0.5–2% range, and a real pay rate. Put those into the break-even table above and the decision to scale makes itself.

Week 3+

Re-book the winners, drop the rest, add ranks 11–20

Creator marketing is won by repeat posts from the few who work, not by one post from many. Expect two or three of the ten to carry the campaign — pay those three more and stop paying the others.

I have not contacted anyone

The list, the prices and the briefs are drafts for you to send. Negotiating and paying creators is your call, not mine.
