01 — the offer

$20 a referral is affordable. Here's the arithmetic.

$20 against $71.99 of year-one revenue

That's 28% of the first year, paid once, after the referred person's payment clears. You keep $52 in year one and all of it thereafter. Nothing is at risk before revenue exists.

Google's implied cost for the same paying customer is $1,335. This is roughly 67× cheaper — and it only ever costs you anything when it works.

What 50 creators is actually worth

Combined reach is 2.29M. Even at a deliberately pessimistic 0.1% of reach trying Boom and 2% of those upgrading, that's ~43 subscribers — about $3,100 of year-one revenue for $860 in bounties. The upside case is an order of magnitude higher, and the downside case costs you nothing but the DMs.

Why these 50 and not any 50. The filter wasn't follower count. It was: does this person already publish screen recordings? Every one of them was found because they posted a video of their screen — a demo, a devlog, a build walkthrough, a tool tutorial.

That matters because you are not asking them to make new content. You're asking them to make the content they already make, with your recorder, and take a link. The behaviour change is close to zero, which is the only kind of creator ask that actually gets done.

Why the offer leads with "free Premium, no strings"

Leading with the bounty makes it an affiliate pitch, and creators delete affiliate pitches. Leading with a genuine free upgrade makes it a gift, and the bounty becomes a reason to stay rather than a reason to reply. You also get the thing you actually need first — a good creator using the product and telling you where it breaks.

04 — Boom Match

You're right about the memory game — and it solves a different problem

This is the strongest instinct in the thread so far, but it belongs in a separate funnel from the 50 above, and it's worth being precise about why.

A memory game is natively the format. A round is fifteen seconds. “Can you beat this?” is the most replicable hook that exists — it produces duets, stitches, quote-tweets and comment-your-score without you making anything else. And a cash prize turns the score reveal into a watch-to-the-end payoff.

The part that makes it specifically yours: gameplay is a screen recording. Boom films Boom Match. Every clip you post is simultaneously game content and a live demonstration of the recorder — the auto-zoom following the cards, the captions, the instant link. You have been circling a loop where the product makes its own marketing, and this is the cleanest version of it in the whole business.

The trap, said plainly

Memory-game content reaches an enormous, cheap, low-intent audience. Most of those viewers will never pay $71.99 for a screen recorder. If you judge this campaign on subscriptions it will look like a failure at a million views.

Except your free tier is ad-supported — so it isn't a trap

A free user who plays and never records still earns you ad revenue. That reframes the “wrong” audience as the right audience for the other revenue line. Judge Boom Match content on free signups, sessions per week and impressions per user. Judge the 50 creators on subscriptions. Two funnels, two scoreboards, one product.

Format · the core loop

“Can you beat 8 seconds?”

A 15-second round, screen-recorded in Boom, ending on the score. Caption invites the reply. The comments become the next week's content, and the leaderboard becomes a reason to come back.

Infinitely repeatable · zero production cost · the recording is the demo
Format · the payoff

The prize reveal

Daily cash prizes already exist in the product. Film the winner being paid. This is the payout-receipt format, and it is the single most-shared genre on short-form — with the advantage that yours is real.

Needs the anti-fraud work first; a fake-looking receipt kills the brand faster than no receipt
Format · the crossover

Creators play, on their own channel

The same 50 people. A memory-game challenge is an easier ask than “try my recorder” — it takes them 30 seconds, it performs well for them, and their audience meets Boom through the game rather than a pitch.

Same recruits, lower-friction first ask — this is how the two funnels actually join
One thing I couldn't verify

I found no public Boom Match site, app listing or link from boomshare.ai — only the “Prize entries” row on your pricing table. So I can't tell how much of the game is shipped, where it lives, or whether it's playable outside the dashboard. That determines whether the plays above are a launch or a campaign, and it's the one thing I'd need from you before writing the content calendar.

05 — this week

The sequence

Day 1

Build the referral link and the payout page

A tracked link per creator, a dashboard showing referred upgrades, and a stated payout day. Creators do not chase money — if it isn't visible and automatic, they stop.

Day 1

Fix the 25-video free cap first

Every person these creators send arrives at your pricing page. Right now it shows the same 25-video wall they left Loom over. That single number will undo the outreach.

Days 2–4

DM the 18 under 10k followers

Highest reply rate, lowest ego cost, and they need the $20. Target ~8 accepting the free Premium. Open their qualifying post first and name the thing they built.

Days 5–10

DM the 20 mid-tier, then the 12 large ones

By now you have creators using it and, ideally, one payout screenshot. That evidence is what converts the bigger accounts — approach them with proof, not a proposal.

Ongoing

Track it in the CSV, not in your head

The export has empty dm_sent, replied and joined columns. Fifty is exactly the size where a spreadsheet works and memory doesn't.
