The number that decides everything is step 4

Every other rate here is roughly knowable from industry benchmarks. What share of a memory-game player converts into someone who installs a desktop screen recorder is genuinely unknown — and it swings the answer from 2 recorders to 2,439.

That is the single measurement wave 1 exists to produce. Everything after it is arithmetic.

Why step 4 is so brutal. A person who just played a memory game on TikTok is not a knowledge worker shopping for a screen recorder. The audience overlap is thin — that's the whole reason the campaign reaches so cheaply. You are buying enormous, cheap, low-intent attention, and low intent is exactly what it says.

2% is my base guess and it may be generous.

That's the base-case cost of a Premium subscriber from this campaign, against the implied cost of one from Google Ads. A rounding error apart.

So if the question is "does the game bring us desktop recorder subscribers cheaply" — on these assumptions, no. It brings them at roughly the same terrible price as paid search. The campaign cannot be justified that way and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

But that isn't what it's for

On credits it looks completely different. Base case: 416 credit buyers, ~$4,995 of revenue against $4,550 spent — it washes its face, and every repeat purchase after that is margin. Optimistic is 30× and pessimistic loses almost the whole budget.

And the 111 active recorders at $41 each are then free. $41 per active user of your core product is genuinely good — it only looks bad when you demand they also subscribe.

The right way to score wave 1

Credits pay for it. Recorder installs are the bonus. Premium subs are noise at this volume — don't set a target for them and don't judge the campaign on them.

3 — what would change the answer

Three levers, in order of impact

lever 01 · biggest

Make the game about screens

The install rate is low because a photo memory game has nothing to do with recording. If the board is a screen — a messy desktop, an app UI, a demo — the person who enjoys it is already someone who finds screens interesting. That's a different, better-qualified visitor.

Photos reach further; screens convert better. Wave 1 should test both and find out which trade is worth more.

lever 02

Fix the landing page first

The 25-video free cap and a rewards-first hero are both sitting between this traffic and a signup. Every point of conversion lost there multiplies through all six steps.

This costs nothing and is the cheapest lever on the list.

lever 03

Don't ask for the desktop install

Asking a phone user to install a desktop app is the biggest single drop in the model. Capture the email or account at the game, then bring them back to desktop later by message — rather than trying to cross devices in one session.

4 — honesty

What this model is and isn't

Measured: the 57.8M views and the $4,550 — those come from real creator view counts, already haircut by 50%. And the $1,335 Google benchmark, from real CPC data.

Assumed: every single conversion rate. Six of them multiply together, so the spread between pessimistic and optimistic is over 1,000×. That is not a flaw in the model — it is an accurate description of how little anyone knows before running this once. Treat the base case as a guess with a defensible shape, not a forecast.
