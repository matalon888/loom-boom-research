SEO Is Not
The Only Game

Five companies in this category, five different engines. SEO built one of them. The biggest outcome — Loom's $975M — was built on a product loop, and Atlassian broke that loop in February. That's the opening I've been under-weighting while writing about channels.

So the answer to your question is no

SEO is the engine for the mid-tier of this market, and it's the engine for companies in adjacent, higher-volume categories. It is not how the category's only billion-dollar outcome happened, and it's not how the profitable three-person company happened either.

It looks like the only game because it's the most visible one — you can count a competitor's pages. You can't count their product loop from the outside, which is exactly why I kept writing about channels.

2 — and it's getting worse

The SEO I told you to build is the SEO that's dying

I recommended 200 device how-to pages to farm the 326,500 monthly searches on "how to screen record on X". I should retract most of that.

  • Zero-click searches hit 68% of US queries in early 2026, up from 60% two years ago.
  • When an AI Overview appears, organic CTR falls 58–61%.
  • 82% of B2B tech queries now trigger one.
  • Informational queries take the worst of it — they're the easiest for a machine to answer outright.
  • Small publishers lost 60% of search referral traffic in two years.

"How to screen record on a Dell laptop" is the single most AI-answerable question imaginable. Building 200 pages of that in 2026 is building for a channel in structural decline.

What survives — and it's a different discipline

Being cited rather than clicked. Brands quoted inside an AI Overview earn 120% more clicks per impression than uncited ones, and referrals from ChatGPT convert at 15.9% against 1.76% for Google organic — nine times better, on far smaller volume.

That's a real opportunity, but it isn't "publish more pages." It's being the source an AI reaches for on a specific question — which favours a small number of genuinely authoritative pages over a large number of thin ones.

What I'd still build, and it's small

Roughly 25 pages, all high-intent, none informational: the Loom video downloader, /alternatives/{competitor}, and honest pricing comparisons. These catch people at the moment of switching, they're the queries AI can't satisfy with a summary because the searcher wants a tool, and they cost a week.

That's the floor. It is not a strategy.

3 — the thing I under-weighted

The engine that actually built this category is sitting unguarded

You're right that Screen Recall is largely a different app. It's a real build, and it doesn't make the recorder better at recording. The Work Mask argument is the honest defence — the game is what turns a weekly utility into a daily habit — but that's a bet, not a fact.

Meanwhile there is a third engine that is neither SEO nor social, requires no new product, and is the only one with a billion-dollar precedent in this exact category.

Loom's growth was never marketing. Every recording sent was an advert delivered by a colleague, with a built-in reason to sign up: you wanted to reply the same way. The product distributed itself through ordinary work.

In February 2026 Atlassian deleted the free Creator seats. The recipient now hits a bill instead of a signup. The loop that built the category's biggest company is broken, and nobody has moved into it.

What rebuilding it actually costs

Not media spend. Product work, and not much of it:

  • No login to watch. Loom's top complaint; you can just not do it.
  • Instant playback, short URLs. Both are named failures in the corpus.
  • A "reply with your own Boom" button on every watch page. This is the entire loop in one control — and it's the one Loom never built well.
  • Never charge for viewers. Then say so, loudly, next to Loom's seat billing.

Every one of those is cheaper than a week of the creator campaign, and each one compounds with every recording your existing users already send.

Why this beats both other engines on paper

SEO costs years and is declining. Social costs $4,550 to find out whether low-intent traffic converts at all. The loop is paid for by users you already have, needs no channel, and every competitor is structurally unable to copy it — because charging for viewers is where their revenue comes from.

scale channel 02

Affiliates — ceiling is recruitment

You pay after the money arrives, so risk is zero and the only limit is how many you sign up.

10 affiliates → ~20 payers/mo, $400 cost. 200 → ~400 payers/mo, $8,000. 1,000 → ~2,000 payers/mo, $40,000. Screen Studio did $17,000 of affiliate sales in one month off 50,000 clicks it never paid for upfront.

scale channel 03 · I missed this

Chrome Web Store

Loom's extension has 8,000,000 users. The Web Store is a search engine with buying intent, it ranks by installs and rating, and AI Overviews cannot touch it.

Ranking for "screen recorder" there is free, durable distribution — and it's the one search surface in this whole document that isn't in decline. I spent the acquisition playbook on Google and never mentioned it.

scale channel 04 · I missed this too

Meta — your own research already said so

The Boom Match keyword study concluded "Meta becomes primary volume engine" and capped Google at $3–4K/month, because search intent didn't match product intent.

The same logic applies here. This audience isn't typing into Google — and I analysed Google, TikTok creators and SEO without once costing Meta. That's a genuine gap in what I gave you.

And your email idea is right

Mobile campaign → "send yourself the desktop link" is exactly the bridge. It doesn't acquire anyone on its own, but it multiplies every other channel by rescuing the phone-to-desktop drop that the funnel model says is the biggest single loss. Build it once, it lifts everything above it.

4 — the honest ranking

If I had to order the three

first · highest confidence

Fix the loop

No login to watch, instant playback, short links, a reply button on every watch page, viewers never billed. Product work, days not months, funded by users you already have.

Why first: it's the only engine with a proven billion-dollar outcome in this exact category, it needs no new app and no channel, and the incumbent just abandoned it.

second · cheap floor

25 high-intent pages

The Loom downloader, competitor alternatives, honest pricing comparisons. One week of work. Skip the 200 how-to pages entirely — that traffic is being eaten by AI answers.

Why second: it costs almost nothing and catches people at the exact moment they're switching. But it will never be a differentiator.

third · a bet, not a plan

Screen Recall

Real reach, genuinely uncontested, and the Work Mask gives it a mechanism. But it is a second product, aimed at low-intent traffic, and the funnel model says it can't be justified on recorder subscriptions.

Why third: you're right that it's a different app. Test it at $4,550 because the information is worth that. Don't commit the company to it before the number comes back.

What I got wrong across these documents

I spent most of this project analysing channels, because channels are what you can measure from the outside — sitemaps, CPCs, follower counts. That produced good data and a slightly wrong emphasis.

The strategy memo did identify the broken loop as the structural opening, and then I let it sit behind two campaign documents. Your question put it back where it belongs.

5 — honesty

Evidence versus argument

Evidence: the page counts and revenue figures, all sourced. The AI Overview and zero-click data — 68% zero-click, 58–61% CTR loss, 82% of B2B queries, 60% publisher traffic loss. The February 2026 Atlassian seat change. Loom's own complaint corpus naming login-to-watch, slow playback and long URLs.

Argument: that fixing the loop outperforms the other two. That's reasoning from precedent — Loom did it once, in this category, and the mechanism is understood — but Boom has a fraction of the sending volume Loom had, and a loop with nothing flowing through it does nothing. If your users aren't already sending recordings, fixing the loop fixes nothing, and social becomes more attractive again by default.

So the first thing to check is your own send volume. That number decides which engine is even available to you, and I don't have it.
